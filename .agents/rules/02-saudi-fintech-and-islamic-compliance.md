# Saudi Fintech & Islamic Compliance Rules

## 1. Saudi Salary & Payday Cycle Alignment

- **Payday per household:** salary cycles follow `households.payday_day` (1–31, default **27**, the common Saudi payday). Only a household admin changes it (`PUT /households/{id}/payday`). Never hard-code the 27th in new code.
- **Cycle Identification (`cycle_key`, `YYYY-MM`):**
  - A cycle runs from the payday of one month to the day before the next payday, on the **Asia/Riyadh** calendar date. A payday beyond the month's length is clamped to its last day; payday 1 means the calendar month.
  - With payday > 15 the key names the month the cycle **ends** in (payday 27: 27 Oct → 26 Nov is `2026-11`); with payday ≤ 15 it names the month it starts in.
  - SQL (`get_saudi_cycle_for_timestamp`), Python (`salary_cycle.get_cycle_for_date`) and Dart compute the same key; database triggers fill `transactions.cycle_key` and clients cannot forge it.
- **Client & API Callers Prohibition:**
  - Clients and API callers MUST NOT forge, compute, or send `cycle_key` or `payday_day` query overrides; the database trigger `trg_populate_transaction_cycle_key` and household configuration are the sole authoritative sources of truth.
- **Payday Check-In:**
  - When the household payday arrives, prompt the user with the Payday Check-in Sheet to record their salary, overtime, bonus, and deductions.
  - Offer the 1-tap Salary Distribution Assistant to fund secondary wallets and bank accounts.
- **Cycle Rollover Handling:** the household chooses its rule (`households.rollover_rule`, `GET|PUT /households/{id}/rollover-rule`, admin to change):
  - `send_to_savings` (default): leftover funds are swept into the savings reserve; categories start fresh.
  - `rollover_to_categories`: leftover funds increase each category in the new cycle (`previous_cycle_delta`).
  - `fresh_start`: allocations reset to baseline with no carryover.

## 2. ZATCA Phase 2 E-Invoicing Compliance

- All Saudi electronic receipts must comply with the Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority (ZATCA) Phase 2 e-invoicing standard.
- The app scanner decodes the Base64 TLV (Tag-Length-Value) barcode:
  - **Tag 1:** Seller Name
  - **Tag 2:** VAT Registration Number (15 digits, starting and ending with 3)
  - **Tag 3:** Timestamp (ISO 8601)
  - **Tag 4:** Invoice Total (including VAT)
  - **Tag 5:** VAT Amount (standard 15% VAT calculation)
- Never reject valid ZATCA QR codes; store `qr_code_raw` and extracted structured line items.

## 3. Islamic Family Finance (Nafaqah vs. Roommate Living Groups)

- **Islamic Sharia Nafaqah Principle:**
  - In Islamic jurisprudence, the husband bears complete legal financial responsibility (*Nafaqah*) for housing, food, clothing, and essential family maintenance.
  - **Strict Prohibition:** NEVER impose automatic 50/50 expense splits between husband and wife.
  - If a wife pays for household groceries or expenses out of her personal funds, track it as **Reimbursable Debt** owed by the husband.
- **Family mode rules (owner decision on audit item UI-24, authoritative).** A household is in family mode when its `households.household_type` is `'family'`, chosen at creation; a `'roommates'` household never is. A family with no wife member stays in family mode, so nothing is ever split between the people in it; with no wife there is nothing to classify and no allowance or reimbursement to record. The wives are its members with `household_memberships.financial_role = 'spouse'` (roles are per household; `users.financial_role` only caches the active household's). The husband is the household's explicit Nafaqah provider, `households.nafaqah_provider_user_id` (owner decision 2026-10-21): by default the primary admin, changed by any admin to another non-spouse member (`set_nafaqah_provider`, `PUT /households/{id}/nafaqah/provider`); never inferred from roles or admin rights. Without a provider, new classified expenses are refused. `wife_personal` purchases count against her allowance only, never the category budgets; edits that would leave the reimbursement ledger overpaid are refused (`ledger_would_be_overpaid`).
  1. **Nafaqah is on the husband.** Shared family/household expenses are his responsibility. There is **no 50/50 or any percentage split** between the spouses anywhere in family mode (API, app banners, settlement cards, chat). `split_ratio` and "Shared 50/50" belong to roommate/partner households only.
  2. **Wife pays for something shared** (household, children/dependents, the husband) from her own money → `transactions.nafaqah_class = 'wife_reimbursable'` with `nafaqah_spouse_id` = the wife. It is a debt the husband owes her. The app shows the outstanding balance and history, and records partial or full repayments in the append-only `spouse_reimbursements` ledger (`record_spouse_reimbursement`); paying back more than is outstanding is rejected.
     - **How a repayment moves money (owner decisions 2026-11-06, migration `20261105001300`):**
       - *Who sees and who acts (owner decisions 2026-11-06, reviews R1-01 and R3-01).* **Her husband** is the household's Nafaqah provider while he is a current member who is not a wife; **he is gone** when there is no such member (he left, his account was deleted, or nobody was named). One definition: `nafaqah_ledger_husband_id` in SQL, `FamilyContext.ledger_husband_id` / `husband_gone` in the API.
         - *Seeing.* A wife's ledger (what is owed, the advances behind it, repayments, reversals, her requests and their outcomes, decline reasons and notes) is read by her and by her husband only. A co-wife, an ordinary member and an admin who is not a wife read nothing of it: no rows, no totals, no entry for her in any summary (`wives[]`), settlement or notice; a wife sees only her own entry. When the husband is gone, an admin of the household who is not a wife reads it too.
         - *Acting.* Only her husband records a repayment, approves or declines her request, and reverses; she only makes and cancels her own requests. When he is gone, an admin who is not a wife does what he did. A wife never sees or acts for a co-wife, even as an admin. The account rules are unchanged: a personal account only by its owner, a shared pool only by an admin who is not a wife, so the husband pays from a pool only if he is an admin.
         - *A deleted wife's record* follows the same rule: seen and closed (paid or waived) by the husband it is owed by; by an admin who is not a wife only when he is gone; never by a co-wife or an ordinary member. Her deletion notices and emails go to the same people.
         - The database enforces it (`nafaqah_may_manage_ledgers`, the read policies on the ledger, requests, outcomes and former member tables through `nafaqah_caller_manages_ledgers`, `get_spouse_reimbursement_balance`, `nafaqah_may_repay`, `former_member_debt_require_manager`, the account helper); the API checks the caller on every route (`require_ledger_reader`, `can_view_wife_ledger`, `can_manage_wife_ledgers`, `resolve_wife_id`: a wife resolves herself only) and the settlement totals; the app hides what the caller cannot use. Her allowance is not her ledger (rule 3 says who sees and sets it): an admin who is not a wife and reads no ledger gets each wife's allowance alone (`allowance_wives`).
       - *Every repayment is tracked.* The payer picks the account it leaves: his own personal account, or a shared pool when he is an admin (in the household, not archived, in SAR, holding the amount; a cash payment leaves his Cash account). That account goes down by the amount and her wife account (below) goes up by exactly the same amount, as one `account_transfers` row from his account to hers (`purpose` naming it, so each account's own history shows "Repayment to wife"), linked from the ledger row (`transfer_id`). The ledger row records who actually paid (`paid_by_user_id`), the wife account it credited (`wife_account_id`) and what was owed before and after it. Her own accounts are never credited. No budget or transaction moves: her expense already counted when she recorded it. A call without an account (the old signatures, or an API request without `account_id`) is refused with `app_update_required` (HTTP 426) and records nothing. A request key makes a retry record once and answer as the first time, also after the account it left was deleted (the ledger keeps `account_id`); the same key with another amount, account, kind or person is refused (`request_key_reused`). A key names one operation in its household, whatever the path (review R2-01): every call that takes one (a repayment, an approval, a reversal, her request, a former member's payment, waiver or reversal) claims it in `nafaqah_request_keys` in the same transaction, so a key used on another path, for another wife or on another record is `request_key_reused` and moves nothing, also when two calls race. In the app, a sheet whose answer was lost (no answer, a server failure, or any failure that is not one of the API's definite answers) keeps its key and what it sent, also after it is closed and reopened, until a definite answer (review R2-02).
       - *The wife account (owner decisions 2026-11-06, migration `20261105001300` section 12, superseding "her account is never credited").* An account of kind `wife` (`accounts.account_type = 'wife'`, `assigned_wife_id`): personal (not shared), in SAR, owned by her husband and assigned to one wife of the household. A wife may still have accounts of her own; those stay hers alone and no repayment credits them.
         - *Made* only by her husband (`create_wife_account`, `POST /households/{id}/nafaqah/wife-accounts`; nobody when he is gone), never by a client's own insert (`trg_accounts_wife_account_guard`). *Assigned* to another wife only by him, only while it has no history (`assign_wife_account`, `wife_account_has_history`). *Archived* by its owner (`archive_wife_account`). Nobody changes its kind or its wife by hand.
         - *Used.* She spends from it: her personal purchases (`wife_personal`) and her advances (`wife_reimbursable`, "I paid myself"), as their creator (`trg_wife_account_spending`, the balance trigger's wife branch). He funds it (`transfer_between_accounts` into it, by its owner or her ledger's managers). **He does not spend from it** (owner default): what is in it is hers to spend, so his own expenses never lower her balance; his money leaves his own accounts. Nothing is transferred out of it (`wife_account_no_transfer_out`), by its owner either; the only other way money leaves it is a reversal.
         - *Every repayment credits one of hers.* On a direct repayment and on an approval the payer picks it (he sees all of hers); on her request ("I received X") she picks which of hers it went into (`spouse_reimbursement_requests.wife_account_id`), and the approval credits that one unless he switches to another of hers, never to an account that is not hers (`wife_account_invalid`). She has none that is not archived: refused with `wife_account_required`, and the app offers him "create her wife account". A former member's payment credits nothing (she is gone; her wife account is archived).
         - *A reversal* takes the amount back out of the wife account the repayment credited, into his account, in one transfer row; refused when that account no longer holds the amount (`wife_account_insufficient_funds`, with what it holds) or is archived (`reversal_account_unavailable`). Budgets and her allowance never move with a repayment or a reversal.
         - *Deletion.* When she leaves the household or her account is deleted, her wife accounts are archived and assigned to nobody (a former wife's, the former member pattern): they stay her husband's with their balance and history; her purge deletes her personal purchases (the account's balance stays as it was: the money she spent stays spent) and takes her name out of the account's name. When his account is deleted, the wife accounts he owns pass, with balance and history, to the household's primary admin, or to the wife it is assigned to when that admin is another wife (`account_deletion_wife_account_recipient`); a former wife's account never goes to a wife. The purge's ledger invariant covers their balances. A client cannot delete one a repayment used (`account_has_repayments`).
       - *She asks, he approves.* The wife never records a repayment (`wife_must_request`). She marks "I received X": a pending request (`spouse_reimbursement_requests`) that moves no money and does not lower what is owed; she can cancel it. The husband or an admin who is not a wife approves it from an account (the normal repayment of exactly that amount, re-checked against what is owed at that moment) or declines it with an optional reason (at most 200 Unicode code points). Pending requests together never exceed what is owed: a new one is refused beyond it, and whatever lowers what is owed (a direct repayment, an approval, a smaller advance) closes in the same transaction the pending requests that no longer fit, as `already_repaid`, with nothing moved, keeping them in the order she made them while they fit (so the most recent close first). Each outcome is a row of its own (`spouse_reimbursement_request_outcomes`).
       - *Corrections are reversals.* Nothing is deleted, edited or voided. A reversal (`kind = 'reversal'`, at most one per repayment) returns the full amount to the account the repayment left (a transfer from no account, "Repayment reversed" in that account's history) and adds it back to what is owed; the repayment stays, marked reversed. Refused for a row from before tracking (no transfer) and for an archived or deleted account. Who reverses: her ledger's manager only (her husband; an admin who is not a wife when he is gone; for a former member's record, its manager), from the account it left as the account rules say: a personal account as its owner, a pool as an admin (review R3B-06, owner decision 2026-11-06, superseding R1-07). A replaced husband or a demoted admin no longer reverses, even his own account's repayment, which then nobody reverses (the owner accepts this); a pool's repayment, the husband if he is an admin, or an admin who is not a wife when he is gone (review R3-01). Enforced by `reverse_spouse_reimbursement`, `reverse_former_member_debt_payment` and the API's `can_reverse_repayment`. A client cannot delete an account a repayment or a reversal used (`account_has_repayments`, review R3-05): it is archived instead, so the correction stays possible; the purge still deletes the accounts of the person it deletes.
       - *What is owed* = her advances − repayments + reversals; pending requests never count.
       - *A deleted wife's record* (`former_member_debts`) is paid from an account the same way, with nothing credited ("Paid on a former member's record" in the account's history, with no name); a payment is reversed the same way (a settled record opens again, never after her waiver); her waiver moves nothing; there are no requests. While a record is open the household cannot switch from family to roommates (`set_household_type` names `former_member_debt`; the API answers `former_member_debt_open`, review R2-04 owner decision): it is closed first, by a payment of what remains or her waiver.
  3. **Wife buys something personal for herself** → `nafaqah_class = 'wife_personal'`. Not a debt: it is charged to her personal allowance ("مصروفها") for the salary cycle (`cycle_key`, household payday to the day before the next). The husband (an admin or not) or a household admin sets the amount per cycle (`member_allowances`, `set_member_allowance`), never a wife: not the wife herself, and not a co-wife, even an admin. The app shows allocated / spent / remaining, and overspend shows a negative remainder (never blocked).
     - *Who sees it (owner decision 2026-11-06, migration `20261105001300`).* Her allowance (its rows, allocated / spent / remaining, any figure in a summary) is read by the wife herself (her own only), her husband (the provider while he is a current member who is not a wife, as in rule 2) and a household admin who is not a wife. A co-wife, even an admin, and an ordinary member read nothing of it: no rows, no figures, no entry in any summary. The database enforces it (`member_allowances_select_household` through `nafaqah_caller_sees_allowances` / `nafaqah_may_see_allowances`, `get_member_allowance_status`, `set_member_allowance`), the API checks the caller (`GET|PUT /households/{id}/nafaqah/allowance`: `require_allowance_reader`, `can_view_allowance`, `can_see_allowances`, `can_set_allowance`; the summary's `wives[].allowance` and `allowance_wives`), and the app shows an allowance and its set action only to who may see or set it.
     - *Who sees her personal purchases (owner decision 2026-11-06, migration `20261105001300` section 10d).* Her `wife_personal` transactions are read by the same people as her allowance: she herself (her own), her husband and a household admin who is not a wife. A co-wife (even an admin) and an ordinary member read none of them, nor anything derived from them: amount, merchant, notes, items, receipt photo, the `nafaqah_spouse_id` link, a per-cycle sum or any total that includes them. Every other transaction keeps its household-wide read, her `wife_reimbursable` advances included. Who records, edits or voids them is unchanged; a caller who cannot read a row cannot update or delete it, also by a statement that names no row (review R3B-01: the UPDATE and DELETE policies of `transactions` carry the read test, USING and WITH CHECK, and so does the receipts DELETE policy; the API's own edits, `household_permissions.assert_can_modify_transaction`, answer such a row as not found). Her advance (`wife_reimbursable`) is re-attributed (its wife or class changed) by a client only by her ledger's managers or by her clearing her own: never by a wife-admin, nor by another admin while the husband is there (review R3B-08, `trg_wife_advance_attribution`); its other fields are edited as before. `nafaqah_receipt_hidden` checks membership first and answers a path of nothing like a photo the caller does not read (review R3B-04). Enforced by the database (`transactions_select_household`: the class is tested first, then her own row, then `nafaqah_caller_sees_allowances`; the receipts storage read policy through `nafaqah_receipt_hidden`; Realtime follows the policy), by the API for every answer it builds from transactions with the service role (`nafaqah_service.can_view_personal_purchase` / `visible_transactions`: price history and shopping basket, the monthly statement, recurring bills, the salary cycle list, both settlements, duplicate warnings), and by the app, which only shows what it reads (a row that becomes readable through Realtime is taken in; an edit or delete that reached no row says the record no longer exists). Category budgets never count them. A legacy personal purchase paid from a shared pool leaves the pool's history for those who do not read it; the pool's balance is unchanged and visible to every member.
     - *Her transfers and the plans of her personal purchases (owner decision 2026-11-06, migration `20261105001300` section 10e).* Read by the same people: an `account_transfers` row with either side a personal (not shared) account of a wife of the household (she reads her own accounts' rows; one between two wives' accounts is read by both), and an installment plan linked (`original_transaction_id`) to her personal purchase (its shop, amounts, dates, schedule and payments are the plan's own fields). A co-wife (even an admin) and an ordinary member read neither: not in an account's Activity, a pool's included, and not in the plan list. *The owner of an account always reads its transfers* (owner decision 2026-11-06): a row with her account on one side and someone's own personal account on the other is read by that account's owner too, so a member who sends money from his account to hers, or receives from hers, keeps that row; no one else gains a row, and a shared pool is the household's, so its `owner_user_id` reads nothing more and a pool's history is unchanged. *A plan stays hers after her purchase is gone* (owner decision 2026-11-06): the plan keeps her as its owner (`installment_plans.personal_owner_id`, worked out by the database from the linked purchase, never written by a client), so after her purchase is deleted (the link becomes NULL) or the plan is unlinked it is still read only by her, her husband and the admins who are not wives. While linked, the plan follows its purchase: reclassified from `wife_personal` to an ordinary expense or to her advance, the plan becomes household-wide (also after the purchase is deleted later); an ordinary purchase made hers, or moved to a co-wife, takes its plan with it; a plan linked to another purchase takes that purchase's owner. Existing plans got the owner of their linked purchase once; a plan whose purchase was deleted before `001300` has nothing to learn it from and stays household-wide. Balances never change: a pool's stays read by every member. Repayments never involve her own accounts: they credit her wife account, whose rows follow its own rule (rule 04). A repayment's own transfer rows (`purpose` set: a repayment to the wife, its reversal, a former member's payment, its reversal) are her ledger's (review R3B-02, owner decision 2026-11-06): read only by the wife whose repayment it is, her ledger's managers (for a former member's record, its managers) and the owner of the personal account the money left or returned to (his own rows only); a pool's lines too, so other members see the pool's balance move but not the line. Every other transfer and plan keeps its household-wide read. Enforced by the database (`transfers_select_household` through `account_transfers.personal_owner_ids`, the owners of the personal sides kept on insert, `nafaqah_caller_hidden_wives` and the caller's own id; the installment_plans policy through the transactions read policy and the plan's `personal_owner_id`, kept by `trg_installment_plans_personal_owner` and `trg_transactions_plan_owner`; a deleted person's id leaves both columns (`trg_users_forget_transfer_owner`); Realtime follows both), by the API (`nafaqah_service.can_view_installment_plan`, which also applies the plan's kept owner; the plan list, a payment, an edit and a new plan linked to a purchase answer 404 to anyone else) and by the app, which shows what it reads (a delete that reached no plan says the record no longer exists). When she leaves the household she is no longer a wife of it, so her transfers and plans are read like other rows, as her purchases are. When her account is deleted her plans stay hidden (owner decision 2026-11-06): the purge clears her id from them and marks them a former wife's (`installment_plans.former_member_personal`, which names nobody), read only by her husband and the admins who are not wives; a delete by any other path marks her unlinked plans the same way. The plans read policy compares only what each plan keeps (`personal_owner_id`, the mark) and looks up no purchase (review R3B-03); a plan is created or linked again only to a purchase the caller reads, with one refusal for a purchase she does not read, an id of nothing and another household's purchase (review R3B-05), and nobody, the server included, links a plan to another household's purchase.
  4. **Husband pays for shared things** (or buys something for his wife) → an ordinary household expense: no class, no debt.
  - **Wife Expense Account Isolation (`trg_enforce_wife_class_account`):**
    - Under migration `20261101000100`, wife-class transactions (`wife_personal`, `wife_reimbursable`) CANNOT debit a shared pool or husband's account.
    - They must link exclusively to a personal, non-shared, non-archived account owned by the designated wife (`owner_user_id = nafaqah_spouse_id`), or to the wife account assigned to her (`assigned_wife_id = nafaqah_spouse_id`, migration `20261105001300`), or remain unlinked (`account_id IS NULL`). A wife account carries only her wife-class rows, created by her.
  - A wife's voluntary contribution (Tabarru') is only ever an explicit opt-in flag (`is_tabarru` on a settlement input); it is never assumed or defaulted.
  - Money is `numeric(14,2)` in SQL, `Decimal`/integer halalas in Python. Ledger writes go only through the `SECURITY DEFINER` RPCs with a pinned `search_path` and actor checks; the API passes the verified JWT subject as `p_actor_user_id` and checks the wife belongs to the URL's household (no IDOR).
  - Roommate mode (peers, no spouse) is unaffected: equal splits and multi-roommate debt simplification as below.
- **Shared Living Groups (Roommates / Flatmates):**
  - Flatmates or roommates share joint household living expenses (Rent, Utilities, Internet, Cleaning).
  - Use the Multi-Roommate Settlement Engine with multi-way debt simplification to compute net balances without circular transfers.
  - Support Shared Expense Pools (`is_shared_pool = true`) where roommates contribute their monthly dues and track expenditures against the shared pool.

## 4. Multi-Source Income & Zero-Based Account Balances

- Users maintain real accounts across Banks (Al Rajhi, SNB, Riyad Bank), E-Wallets (STC Pay, Urpay, Tiqmo), Crypto (Binance), and Cash.
- **Operating Cash Single Source of Truth:**
  $$\text{Operating Cash} = \text{Liquid Balance} - \text{Pending Commitments}$$
  - `Liquid Balance` is the exact sum of balances across non-archived, SAR-denominated operating pool accounts.
  - `Pending Commitments` is the sum of unpaid recurring bills and active cycle installment plan obligations.
  - Category *Spent Amount* is strictly EXCLUDED from the operating cash subtraction because the database trigger `trg_update_account_balance_on_transaction` already decrements `accounts.balance` at write time. Python and Flutter must NEVER manually decrement or clamp balances.
- **Zero-Based Budget Invariant:**
  - Total committed budgets for an account must never exceed that account's actual liquid balance.
  - Display dynamic status indicators:
    - `🟢 Zero-Based (100% Allocated)`
    - `🔵 Surplus Buffer (+SAR X.XX)`
    - `🔴 Deficit Shortfall (-SAR X.XX)` with 1-tap `[⚡ Quick Transfer]` shortcut.
